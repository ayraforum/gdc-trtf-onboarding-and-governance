# Reliance records and correction — later-phase questions

## Document control

- Date: 2026-09-24
- Status: Exploratory co-lead guidance; outside the initial issuer framework, not agreed task-force requirements
- Source input: Nair's [India trust-architecture proposal](https://zenodo.org/records/22840405), sections 7–14

## Reliance record

Consider what evidence a consuming institution needs to explain a decision: the source, authority and recognition scope, applicable time, response version and freshness, its policy version, the permitted purpose, and any exception used. The institution retains that record under its own access and retention obligations. A registry supplies attributable information without receiving a centralized history of personal service decisions.

Specify whether the question concerns authority when the credential was issued, authority now, or both. Neither answer alone establishes the current status or accuracy of an individual credential.

## Responsibility and correction

| Role | Question to settle in the applicable arrangement |
| --- | --- |
| Source authority | Who can correct authority or status information, and what effective scope does the correction have? |
| Registry operator | How do corrected answers reach the relevant access paths, and what evidence identifies the period of exposure? |
| Consumer | Who can reconsider an affected decision, and what alternative or interim route is available? |
| Person or organization affected | Where can an error be raised without navigating an unclear chain of operators? |

The paper proposes a reliance safe harbour. Treat it as a legal-design question, not existing protection or a common framework requirement. Each party's responsibility needs its own applicable basis; a recorded assertion about liability does not create that basis.

## Issues to resolve before specifying requirements

- Retention sufficient for accountability versus unnecessary personal-data accumulation.
- Correction propagation and cache expiry without monitoring people's subsequent transactions.
- A source unavailable during a decision versus an authoritative negative answer.
- Signed information that is authentic but stale, replayed, or incomplete.
- Consumer-purpose restrictions without prescribing wallets or credential-delivery mechanisms.

Keep these questions out of the current framework's internal-lifecycle exclusions. See the [Track 1 comparison](../references-and-inputs/nair-india-architecture-comparison-2026-09-24.md).
